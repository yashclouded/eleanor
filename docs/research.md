# Eleanor research audit

Reviewed 19 October 2026 · evidence, limitations, and design implications

This is a targeted technical and learning-science review of the original brief, not a preregistered systematic review or a clinical assessment. It checks the brief’s central citations, seeks contrary findings and boundary conditions, and adds sources for measurement, accessibility, and implementation. Product decisions live in [doc.md](../doc.md); their implementation sequence lives in [execution.md](execution.md). Sections 1–6 audited the v1 pilot design; section 7 adds the sources behind the v2 hosted, rated product.

## 1. Conclusions that the evidence can support

1. **Optimize for subsequent independent performance.** Immediate AI-assisted success and learning are different outcomes. There is experimental evidence of both harmful and beneficial assistance designs.
2. **Teach concrete mechanisms.** Domain-specific instruction, feedback, retrieval, and opportunities to apply an idea are defensible ingredients. Their presence does not validate this product or a general “thinking ability” score.
3. **Test rather than assume transfer.** A surface-changed twin is a useful near-transfer task. It is not independent evidence of better real-world decisions or broad cognitive enhancement.
4. **Measure confidence carefully.** Accuracy, completion, task difficulty, confidence bias, reliability, and a proper scoring rule answer different questions. A single headline score would hide important failures.
5. **Use the smallest intervention that can be evaluated.** Reviewed content and authored support can test the central mechanism without generative models, latent-trait estimation, or three different game engines.

The strongest unresolved question is whether adults will return to this particular practice and improve on fresh, independently assessed reasoning tasks. None of the sources below answers that for Eleanor.

### Review method and access limits

Searches covered the cited authors/titles and primary publisher, author, university, arXiv, and official software/documentation pages. Additional searches covered proper scoring, retrieval practice, cognitive forcing, targeted debiasing in field settings, and competing product descriptions. News stories, social posts, and summaries were not used as scientific authorities.

The register distinguishes **full text** from **abstract/indexed text**. Some Nature, Science, and PMC pages returned access challenges; indexed publisher passages and author/repository copies were used where available. This review did not reproduce study analyses, inspect every supplement, audit raw datasets, or independently grade every risk of bias. Effect estimates are the authors’ reported estimates, not pooled estimates calculated here. Publication status refers to the linked version; absence of a journal reference on arXiv does not prove that no other publication exists.

## 3. Corrections to the original brief

| Original direction | Revised interpretation or action |
| --- | --- |
| “Answer-giving AI damages independent thinking, fast.” | Certain randomized interventions lowered later unaided performance on their tested tasks. Long-term general damage is not established. |
| Direct-answer users declined while hint users did not. | Liu’s usage subgroups were not randomized to those behaviors. Separate this association from the randomized AI-access effect. |
| Persistence loss is consistent across all tests. | Experiment 2’s aggregate skip difference was not statistically significant. Keep persistence exploratory and distinguish it from accuracy. |
| The tutor “removed most of the harm.” | Guardrails largely mitigated the measured deficit in one field trial; lack of a significant deficit is not proof of learning gains or equivalence everywhere. |
| Think first, then AI, “every time.” | Default to an independent task attempt, while permitting prerequisite instruction and an honest give-up. Productive failure has meaningful boundary conditions. |
| Calibration is the headline Brier score. | Brier is a proper loss influenced by more than calibration. Present it with reliability, accuracy, completion, difficulty, and sample size. |
| A timer establishes genuine effort. | Time does not establish engagement. Keep timers optional; do not delay accessibility or appropriate help. |
| Twins measure “real learning.” | They measure retention/application on closely related tasks. Add separate held-out assessments and reserve far-transfer claims. |
| Surface renaming creates a transfer item. | Vary representation and context while reviewing the shared mechanism and prerequisites. Do not count exact repeats as novel transfer. |
| Twelve skills can be rated from onboarding. | Treat the map as a proposed taxonomy. Report observations on measured tasks; defer psychometric claims and multidimensional fitting. |
| Paste events and tab switches identify assistance. | These are ambiguous and privacy-invasive proxies. Remove passive suspicion scoring; use voluntary declarations and low-stakes incentives. |
| LLM receives the solution, so it cannot invent a wrong path. | Correct context is not a correctness guarantee. Use reviewed hints and deterministic grading; evaluate generated debriefs separately. |
| Embedding distance measures distinctiveness. | It may capture irrelevant differences, dialect, or nonsense. Keep it exploratory and require human-reviewed quality and validity evidence. |
| Start three arenas together. | First validate one numerical-judgment loop. Add chess and Rule Lab through separate gates. |
| Collect content, then run baseline. | A participant who authored or reviewed solutions has already trained on that content, including its twins. Use independently prepared practice and assessments, or label the period usability-only. |
| Alternate hint and direct-answer weeks for N=1. | Learning carries over; order, content, and expectation effects confound that comparison. Use descriptive self-tracking first and a later parallel randomized study. |
| Build in two weeks and use it 12 of those same 14 days. | Separate the engineering interval from baseline and the 14-day observation interval. |
| A 30–50-person beta can demonstrate efficacy. | Use it to estimate feasibility, attrition, outcome variability, and possible effects. Plan a powered follow-up before making causal product claims. |
| Publish a preprint after 100 users. | Publish when protocol, consent, outcome quality, and analysis support a useful honest report; user count alone is insufficient. |

## 4. Landscape check

Official product descriptions were checked on 19 October 2026. They establish advertised features, not independent learning effectiveness, full feature parity, or pricing. This is a small reference set, not a systematic market study.

| Reference | Observed overlap | What Eleanor still has to demonstrate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Brilliant](https://brilliant.org/faq/) | Interactive STEM learning by doing. | A reason to return beyond established interactive curricula, and measurable benefits of the independent-commitment/twin loop. |
| [Khanmigo](https://www.khanmigo.ai/) | Guided reasoning and an explicit stated aim to avoid simply giving learners answers. | The brief’s claim that existing AI tutors all optimize for answers is too broad. Eleanor needs its own advantage and evaluations. |
| [Lichess practice](https://lichess.org/practice) | Structured practice of chess concepts with established board infrastructure. | Merely adding chess is not differentiation. Reuse the pattern and assess the added learning interaction. |

The credible initial position is “a private, finite practice with visible evidence about your independent work.” Demand for that package, willingness to pay, and comparative effectiveness remain untested. Accounts, subscriptions, and marketing spend would not resolve those questions.

## 6. Questions the first experiments must answer

| Hypothesis | How to challenge it | Failure response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adults find the work worth repeating. | Observe actual sessions, nonreturn reasons, perceived burden, and optional comments. | Improve the problems and session length before adding engagement rewards. |
| Users distinguish an answer probability from confidence in its correctness. | Ask pilot users to explain both inputs; inspect extreme/default patterns. | Change labels and onboarding before interpreting confidence charts. |
| Reviewed twins preserve the intended skill while requiring fresh work. | Independent review, error patterns, actual delays, and performance across representations. | Rewrite families; do not fix the issue by relabeling repetition as transfer. |
| The commitment step supports learning at acceptable cost. | Track completion and burden; later randomize a matched comparison. | Reduce friction and add prerequisite support; preserve measurement integrity. |
| Feedback changes subsequent independent application. | Separate held-out assessment, twins, and supported revisions. | Revise content and consolidation; avoid attributing novelty or task familiarity to transfer. |
| Optional model dialogue improves the authored debrief. | Keep the authored loop as baseline; evaluate the model’s added feedback and then compare outcomes. | Remove the model if it adds errors, leakage, expense, or no useful benefit. |

Claims may expand only with corresponding evidence: from “practised this mechanism,” to “improved on these held-out tasks,” to “outperformed this comparison under this protocol.” General professional, educational, cognitive-health, or IQ benefits would need their own evidence and are outside the current proposal.

| S10 · Brown & Burton (1978), "Diagnostic models for procedural bugs in basic mathematical skills", *Cognitive Science*, 2(2), 155–192 | Systematic wrong answers can be traced to identifiable faulty procedures. | Developed for arithmetic procedures. Eleanor's "mistake fingerprints" apply the idea to authored misconception values, and each one must be reviewed so it does not mislabel a learner's actual method. |
| S11 · Kluger & DeNisi (1996), "The effects of feedback interventions on performance", *Psychological Bulletin*, 119(2), 254–284 | Feedback improves performance on average, but a substantial share of interventions *reduced* it, especially when attention shifted from the task to the self. | Feedback copy must be task- and method-focused, never about the person (Global Constraints in execution.md). A rating shown after every problem could pull attention to the self, so the default reveals rating changes at the end of a set. |
| S12 · Hattie & Timperley (2007), "The power of feedback", *Review of Educational Research*, 77(1), 81–112 | Effective feedback answers where the learner is going, how they are doing, and where to go next, at the task, process, and self-regulation levels. | It informs the result screen, the repair ladder, and the set summary's "next step". It is not evidence for Eleanor's particular wording. |
| S13 · Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber & Redelmeier (1993), "When more pain is preferred to less: adding a better end", *Psychological Science*, 4(6), 401–405 | Retrospective evaluations of an experience are strongly influenced by its end (the peak-end effect). | The study concerned discomfort, not learning sessions. Easing the last problem of a set is an inference to test against return rates. It must never inflate ratings; it changes only which problem is selected. |
| S14 · Eleanor ERM-1 simulation (internal, 20 October 2026; reproduced as a gate in [execution.md, task 1.5](execution.md#task-15-model-simulation-gate)) | Under synthetic, well-specified conditions, ratings converge (median error about 40 after 60 responses), the realised success rate matches the 70% target, and item difficulties are recovered. | It is synthetic data generated by the same logistic family as the model. It checks mechanics only. Rapid population-wide learning produced roughly 100 points of scale drift, which is why anchors, model health, and later recalibration exist. |

### What these sources change and what they leave open

- **Rated adaptive practice is established engineering.** Its value for adult *reasoning* is not. The primary evidence will come from held-out checkpoints and echo results, analysed as in section 6.
- **Public leaderboards are the least-supported v2 choice.** S05's evidence is mixed in scope, and leaderboards create incentives to use AI on rated problems. They ship because the owner chose them, with the safeguards in doc.md §9. They stay under review: if integrity flags or learner feedback show harm, narrow them first (for example, to new-peak or club boards).
- **No source supports claims of general cognitive improvement.** The claims policy in doc.md §1 and section 1 of this audit still applies.
